## Coffee Guide

### What is espresso?
Espresso is a small, concentrated coffee made by forcing hot water through finely ground coffee at high pressure. At Bean Scene we pull our espresso at nine bars of pressure for twenty-five seconds. Espresso is the base of cappuccinos, macchiatos and lattes.

### Cappuccino vs latte vs macchiato
All three start with espresso. A cappuccino has espresso, steamed milk and a thick layer of foam, so it tastes balanced and airy. A latte has more steamed milk and only a thin layer of foam, so it is milder and creamier. A macchiato is espresso "marked" with a small amount of milk, so it is the strongest of the three.

### Cold brew vs iced coffee
Iced coffee (like our Iced Latte and Iced Cappuccino) is espresso or hot coffee chilled and served over ice. Cold brew is made by steeping coffee in cold water for many hours, usually overnight, which makes it smoother, less acidic and usually stronger in caffeine.

### Coffee roasts
Light roasts are brighter and more acidic and keep more of the bean's original flavour. Medium roasts are balanced and sweet. Dark roasts are bold and smoky. Our beans are slow-roasted in small batches until the sugars turn to caramel, giving a rich, sweet, smoky depth.

### Arabica vs robusta
Arabica beans are usually grown at high altitude and taste sweeter and smoother, with more complex flavours. Robusta beans are hardier, more bitter and contain roughly twice the caffeine. Our beans are hand-picked from high-altitude farms.

### Making good coffee at home
Use freshly roasted beans and grind them just before brewing. Use filtered water just off the boil (about 90–96°C). A common ratio is about 1 gram of coffee to 15–17 grams of water. Clean your equipment regularly.

### Storing coffee beans
Keep beans in an airtight container away from light, heat and moisture, at room temperature. Avoid the fridge, which adds moisture and odours. Whole beans are best within a few weeks of roasting; ground coffee loses flavour faster.

### Coffee and health
For most healthy adults, up to about 400 mg of caffeine a day (around four cups of brewed coffee) is generally considered safe. Pregnant people and those sensitive to caffeine should have less and may want to ask a doctor. Black coffee has almost no calories; milk, chocolate and sugar add calories.
